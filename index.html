The chemistry behind it

Dissolved minerals are ions. Sodium, calcium, magnesium, bicarbonate, chloride and sulfate set the taste and the total dissolved solids (TDS). Your gut, kidneys and blood pressure each respond to specific ions: sodium holds water in the body, magnesium with sulfate draws water into the intestine (a laxative effect), and nitrate can interfere with oxygen transport in infants. pH shows how acidic or alkaline the water is, which matters for reflux and for taste.

The limits used here come from the WHO drinking-water guidelines and common low-sodium and infant-feeding advice. Rules are simplified.

Important: this is a screening and learning tool, not a medical device or diagnosis. It cannot detect germs, pesticides or heavy metals unless you have test values for them. If you have a medical condition or react to water, check with a doctor.

Methods and sources

Each result compares a measured value with a guideline limit, then tightens the limit for the sensitivities you tick. Limits marked WHO come from the WHO Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality. Limits marked screening are conservative rules of this tool, not official standards.

About this project

Good Water Reader is a chemistry and chemical engineering project that matches the dissolved ions in drinking water to a person’s body. Some people react to waters that most people drink without harm, so a single “safe” limit is not enough. The method compares each substance with WHO guideline values and adjusts the limits for sensitivities such as low-sodium diets, kidney concerns, infants and sensitive stomachs.
